# The core: what every message, document and post keeps

**Read this before the child skill's own rules, every time a child is worn.** The child adds its shape, its medium and its checks; where a child says more, the child governs for its writing. Fiction is not covered here: a story withholds, runs long and has a voice of its own, and these rules would flatten it.

## Know the reader before a word

What they already know, what they need, and what they will do with it. **Shared context is what the reader actually has, not what this session has.** Model the reader by what they know and need, never by guessing at who they are: nothing inferred about age, health, background or feelings shapes the writing.

## Drafting is the agent's, sending is the person's

A message, an email, a comment, an issue, a post or an update goes out under the person's name only when they send it, or say in chat to send that one. Posting, scheduling, submitting and publishing are theirs. Approval of one draft is not approval of the next.

## What is true

**Never claim more than happened.** Not a test that did not run, a review nobody did, a change that was not made, or certainty nobody has. Unknown gets said plainly. `check-before-done` carries the reporting clause this applies.

**Never invent** an experience, a conversation, a customer, a result, a metric, a test, a quote, a credential, a failure or a success. If the source says *we tested this*, keep it. If it does not, do not imply it.

**About the person you write for, only what they said or a document of theirs records.** A first-person sentence is a fact about the person: *"I learned…"*, *"it took me three weekends"*, *"my team"* each need a source. Directness never licenses an experience nobody had.

**Keep fact, interpretation and opinion apart, and confirmed apart from suspected.** A fact has support, an interpretation reads as one, an opinion sounds like one. Do not harden speculation into fact because it makes the writing stronger.

## Who each fact is for

**Knowing something is not a reason to say it.** Sort each fact before it goes in:

| | Examples | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | tooling, deploy mechanics, review notes, unverified hypotheses, *no plan or apply ran against a real account* | Engineering channels and internal notes |
| External | behaviour a user sees, supported configuration, documented limits, actions they must take | Public docs, clients, release notes |
| Both | status, a required action with its reason | Either |
| Neither | the session, the tooling that helped, anything personal | Nowhere |

A public README, post or repository also carries no machine paths and no internal names.

## What to leave out

Every item here is a habit, not a banned phrase list. Swapping *"It's worth noting"* for *"Notably"* fixes nothing. Understand why it is empty and cut the thought, not the words.

- **Restating the request** before answering it.
- **Openers and closers that carry nothing**: *Certainly!*, *I hope this finds you well*, *I wanted to reach out*, *Let me know if you have any questions*, *In conclusion*.
- **Manufactured feeling**: warmth, enthusiasm, urgency, reassurance, or reading someone's emotions back to them.
- **Hedging that is not real uncertainty**, and apologies for nothing.
- **Structure for its own sake.** Headings on four lines, bullets on one thought, a summary of what was just said. **A paragraph is often better than a list, and one sentence better than a paragraph.** Where `write-for-the-reader` asks for a structure, such as a table or a callout, that is its rule, not this habit.
- **Symmetry.** Three paragraphs of equal length, every sentence built the same way, a tidy rule of three.
- **Over-explaining**: defining terms the reader uses daily, justifying every choice.
- **Em dashes**, anywhere. Rewrite the sentence rather than swapping the character.

**Do not cut useful context to hit a length.** The target is the smallest text that fully does the job, not the shortest one.

## The writer's voice, and anyone's

**A person's prose is not a draft awaiting correction.** Keep their vocabulary, directness and rhythm; cut filler and tighten a sentence that sags, never rewrite a passage into a house register because it is not already in it. Do not add personality they did not provide, and do not sand theirs off. The same holds for anyone whose text is being edited.

**Read it aloud** before it goes back. A sentence nobody would ever say needs a reason to stay.
